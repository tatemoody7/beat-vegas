## 1. Florida law on giving college-football picks

**Recommendation.** Free public picks and free picks to friends are ordinary speech about a lawful activity: no Florida statute licenses, registers or regulates handicappers, and the Gaming Control Commission's materials never mention advice. Paid picks are not "gambling" under chapter 849 either, but they have no safe harbour and sit next to a felony statute whose "influences in any manner" clause has not been tested against tipsters — **LAWYER** before charging. Point every pick at Hard Rock Bet only, never at an offshore book, and say "Florida, 21+" on anything public.

- **No tout statute.** The 2025 chapter 849 index (849.01–849.46) has no section on advice, information, tips, touts, handicapping, forecasting or advertising. Searches for Florida tipster/handicapper licensing found nothing. Nevada — the comparison everyone cites — tried in 2019 (SB 46 would have had the Gaming Commission register "tout services", defined as anyone who "for any form of compensation, fee or remuneration, provides advice or opinions ... related to wagering on racing or sporting events"); Senate Amendment 12 struck it ("Deleted by amendment") and the enrolled chapter contains the word "tout" zero times. I found no US state that registers handicappers today (not an exhaustive 50-state check).
- **The felony that sits nearby.** § 849.14: whoever "stakes, bets, or wagers ... upon the result of any trial or contest of skill ... or whoever aids, or assists, or abets, or influences in any manner in any of such acts ... commits a felony of the third degree" (history ends at ch. 2021-271; no 2025/2026 change). It reaches *unlawful* bets. Sports wagers by players in Florida are lawful only through the Tribe: § 285.710 deems them "exclusively conducted by the Tribe where the servers ... are located", and the Commission's FAQ says "Unless you are placing a wager through sportsbooks operated by or in conjunction with the Seminole Tribe of Florida, you are most likely placing an illegal wager." So a pick aimed at Hard Rock Bet influences a lawful act; a pick a reader takes to an offshore book is the exposure. **LAWYER**: whether "influences in any manner" has ever been read to reach a tipster whose reader bets illegally.
- **Bookmaking is taking bets, not advising.** § 849.25(1)(a): "'bookmaking' means the act of taking or receiving, while engaged in the business or profession of gambling, any bet or wager".
- **The Commission.** § 285.710 designates the Florida Gaming Control Commission "as the state compliance agency having the authority to carry out the state's oversight responsibilities under the compact". Its site and FAQ cover illegal gambling, pari-mutuels, cardrooms and the compact; nothing on advice, handicappers or advertising by third parties.
- **The compact.** Part III.CC defines Sports Betting to include "any collegiate sport or athletic event (but not including proposition bets on such collegiate sport or event)". Part IV deems mobile wagers "to take place exclusively where received at the location of the servers" on Indian lands. Part V limits Sports Betting to "natural persons who are twenty-one (21) years of age or older". Part V.D.9: "The Tribe shall assure that advertising and marketing ... contain a responsible gambling message and a toll-free help-line number ... and that such advertising and marketing make no false or misleading claims" — an obligation on the Tribe, not on third parties. Marketing partners are "Qualified Pari-mutuel Permitholders" paid 60% of the referred net win (Part III.CC.1); there is no affiliate, tout or information-provider clause.
- **Advertising a sportsbook — watch 2027.** CS/CS/CS/HB 189 (2026) would have made non-Seminole "Internet sports wagering" a misdemeanor and promoting "illegal gambling ... Internet gambling, or Internet sports wagering" a third-degree felony, with a carve-out for "gaming activities authorized under s. 285.710(13) and conducted pursuant to a gaming compact". The House laid HB 189 on the table on 2026-03-11 in favour of CS/CS/SB 1580, which "Died in returning Messages" on 2026-03-13. Nothing passed; a refiled version would make promoting any book but Hard Rock a felony while leaving Hard Rock promotion exempt.
- **Consumer protection.** § 501.204 (FDUTPA): "unfair or deceptive acts or practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce are hereby declared unlawful", construed with "great weight" to FTC interpretations. This, not gambling law, is where a paid picks service is most exposed (section 7).
- **Federal layer, advice only.** Wire Act 18 U.S.C. § 1084(a) applies to "Whoever being engaged in the business of betting or wagering" and covers "information assisting in the placing of bets or wagers"; § 1084(b) exempts "news reporting of sporting events" and information sent between places where the betting is legal. A secondary source says a court held the Act did not reach a seller of horse-race predictions who took no bets; the article would not load and the case name is unconfirmed — treat as unverified. UIGEA 31 U.S.C. § 5362 defines "bet or wager" as "the staking or risking by any person of something of value" and "unlawful internet gambling" as "to place, receive, or otherwise knowingly transmit a bet or wager"; publishing picks is neither. Risk low; **LAWYER** only if readers in other states are ever charged.

## 5. Newsletter and paid-tier platforms

**Recommendation.** The binding constraint on a paid tier is payment rails and platform policy, not gambling law. For the free newsletter: Substack's guidelines do not mention gambling and sports-picks Substacks exist; Buttondown bans "promoting gambling" in one line and should be asked in writing first; Mailchimp and Resend prohibit "gambling services or products" outright — do not build on them. For a paid tier: Stripe — which processes Substack and Buttondown — prohibits "Sports forecasting or odds-making with a monetary or material prize"; Gumroad bans picks by name; Patreon allows sports speculation only when "no financial reward is involved"; Whop is the one platform whose policy affirmatively allows paid picks. Get a written answer from Stripe (or the chosen platform) before charging — **LAWYER** optional, the platform answer is what matters.

- **Stripe** (updated 2026-09-22), prohibited businesses: "Sports forecasting or odds-making with a monetary or material prize"; "Games of chance including gambling, internet gambling, casino games, sweepstakes and contests, and fantasy sports leagues with a monetary or material prize"; "'Get rich quick' schemes"; "Businesses that make outrageous claims, use deceptive testimonials". Whether a flat subscription for picks is "forecasting ... with a monetary or material prize" is ambiguous (the phrase reads like contests); Stripe decides, and Substack "built its payments system on Stripe" with Stripe Connect; Buttondown "only supports Stripe natively right now".
- **Substack** Content Guidelines (updated 2026-09-29): no gambling clause. Bans content that "promotes harmful or illegal activities" and "publications whose primary purpose is to advertise external products or services, drive traffic to third party sites, distribute offers and promotions" (an affiliate-funnel newsletter would fail this). Publisher Agreement (updated 2026-07-20): "You agree to process payments from Readers only in the manner determined by us"; content must not be "fraudulent, deceptive". Live examples of picks newsletters carry "entertainment purposes only" disclaimers.
- **Patreon** Community Guidelines (no date shown): creators "may not use Patreon to access, provide, or facilitate gambling for money or an exchange of value"; permits "speculation about sports outcomes" where "no financial reward is involved". A paid tier of picks is the grey zone; free analysis is fine.
- **Gumroad** (revised 2026-09-16): prohibits "gambling (including ... sports forecasting and odds making)"; its clarification names "picks, predictions, tips or 'signals', live odds feeds, sportsbook affiliate and promo-code funnels ... and any promise by the seller that the buyer will make money"; permits "educational materials about gambling strategy, bankroll management, odds calculators ... and bet tracking tools". Out for picks; fine for a tracker.
- **Buttondown** AUP (no date shown): "default stance is to support all content so long as it is not harassing, harmful or otherwise damaging"; lists "Promoting gambling, or online casinos" as prohibited (under its link-farm section). Whether our own picks are "promoting gambling" is their call — ask.
- **Mailchimp** AUP (updated 2025-09-26): "Gambling services or products" prohibited; "day trading tips, and stock market related content" merely scrutinised. Third-party reports describe suspensions without warning.
- **Resend** AUP (updated 2026-08-27): "Gambling services or products" and "Get rich quick opportunities" prohibited.
- **Whop** (read 2026-10-06): prohibits "Betting platforms, wagering operations, lotteries ... or other gambling activities that violate state or federal law" and "Excludes information, analysis, picks, or other advisory services related to gambling or sports betting" from that prohibition. - **Age gating.** Florida law and the compact set 21+ for betting (§ 285.710; Compact Part V). No platform policy read here requires a 21+ gate on picks *content*; the compact's responsible-gambling line and helpline (1-833-PLAYWISE is the Florida number Hard Rock lists) bind the Tribe, not us, but cost nothing.

- **Action Network BetSync** (article dated 2026-09-29): free; imports bets after you connect a sportsbook "through the Action app"; "BetSync availability depends on the sportsbook, the bettor's location, and Action Network integrations" — "Check the list inside the Action app"; Hard Rock Bet / Florida not confirmed. - **Pikkit**: "BookSync is read-only" — you "log in to your favorite sportsbooks" in the app; "30+ supported sportsbooks, DFS apps, and prediction markets"; no manual entry, so "all statistics are verified on the platform"; free with a Pro tier; Hard Rock Bet not confirmed on the public pages read.

## 7. Advertising and consumer protection (FTC, FDUTPA)

**Recommendation.** Treat the record as an advertising claim. Publish the whole record since inception, losses included, in units with its interval, and never a window, a streak or a "lock". Never say "guaranteed", "inside information" or "low-risk" — those three phrases are what every prosecuted tout said. If an affiliate or referral link ever appears, disclose it next to the link. Our ledger design already satisfies this; the risk is in copy, not in data.

- 16 CFR § 255.1(a): "Endorsements must reflect the honest opinions, findings, beliefs, or experience of the endorser ... an endorsement may not convey any express or implied representation that would be deceptive if made directly by the advertiser." § 255.2(b): an endorsement about results "will likely be interpreted as representing that the endorser's experience is representative of what consumers will generally achieve"; without substantiation the advertiser must "clearly and conspicuously disclose the generally expected performance". A published record is the seller's own results claim; the same logic applies — the full record with its interval is the "generally expected performance". § 255.5: a connection "that might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement ... must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously" (money, free product, referral benefits). Testimonials from subscribers would fall under § 255.2 and the 2024 Reviews rule (16 CFR Part 465, effective 2024-10-21: no fake or bought reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, or suppression of negative ones).
- Florida mirrors this through § 501.204 (section 1).
- Enforcement pattern: no FTC action against a sports handicapper was found in 2019–2025 searches; the cases are DOJ wire/mail fraud. Cory Zeidman (plea 2024-12-04, ~$25M, 2004–2020) sold picks on claimed inside information from "doctors" and "television executives", framed betting as "a low or no-risk proposition", and supplied information that "was either fictitious or obtained from an internet search". The trade press notes "it's a common practice for touts to publish cherry-picked predictions ... or even fabricate their records."
- Map to the ledger: full-record-only (Results opens on the bet ledger; every rate carries its Wilson interval; "modelled from the ledger, not reconciled") ✓; units not dollars ✓; CLV sign explained ✓; no deletes after kickoff ✓; add: a one-line "not advice, 21+, Florida only, past results do not predict" footer on every public surface and on every email.
