## Topic 1: goals, limits, and the stop rule (closed 2026-10-09)

**The goal, in one line: prove, with small real stakes at Hard Rock, that a strategy beats its
prices — judged by line value against the market close; 2026 is research, 2027 confirmation.**

Tate's decisions:
1. **Goal.** Betting profit is the only outcome that would, on its own, justify 10+ hours a week
   after January. Research credibility, a business, or a portfolio piece would not on their own.
2. **Target.** Proof is enough: any demonstrated profit. The money is a scoreboard, not income.
3. **Venue.** Hard Rock only, even if the evidence points elsewhere.
4. **Bankroll.** Under $1,000, so bets stay about $10-25.
5. **Timeline.** Two seasons is fine: 2026 research, 2027 confirmation, scale only after.
6. **Proof.** Line value first (a registered strategy passes its closing-price test), then real
   tickets show the same in 2027. This is the current roadmap's design.
7. **Personal ledger.** A profit attempt; keep it as is (top 5 a week, $10), knowing the −5% read below.
8. **Stop rule.** Decided in January with the results in hand, not written in advance.

The arithmetic put to Tate before decisions 2-7 (profit = edge × amount wagered):
- At a real +3% edge and about 100 bets a season, $500 a season needs about $170 a bet, $2,000
  about $670, $10,000 about $3,300; a safe bankroll for each (quarter to half Kelly; full Kelly at
  +3% and −110 is 3.3%) is about $10-20K, $40-80K and $200-400K. At $10 bets it is about $30.
- With a real +3% edge, a 100-bet season still ends in a loss about 38% of the time.
- At $10 bets, about 200 tickets over two seasons finish ahead about 25% of the time with no edge
  at all (−4.5% expected value) and about 67% with a real +3% edge. Dollars cannot separate edge
  from luck at this size; line value can, after a few dozen readings (its per-pick spread is about
  4-5%, against about 95% for a win/loss result).
- Hard Rock may limit a winning account "for any or no reason" (terms §1.5); where that line sits
  is unknown.

## Topic 2: the honest odds (closed 2026-10-09)

Where we stand, each with its confidence:
1. **A model built from public team stats will not earn the proof** (high). It ties Hard Rock at
   best, 54 registry rows found nothing it adds, and every pick with a closing reading shows the vig.
2. **Nor will a standing bias** such as always-unders, weather or TV games (high): blind

   literature looks failed.
3. **Hard Rock's own pricing habits are the untested place** (uncertain). Known: its first-half

   first-half line lags the full-game market inside hours. Today's capture cannot answer that
   (2-3 rows per book per game, the books' own timestamps discarded).
4. **Any edge at Hard Rock is fragile**: it can limit at will, and laziness gets fixed.

My estimate (judgement, not data): about 1 in 10 that some strategy earns the proof by the end of

The test every candidate must pass: *who loses the other side, and why has Hard Rock not fixed it?*

Tate's decisions:

## Topic 3: is the testing machine sound? (closed 2026-10-09)

**Mostly yes; it is the strongest part of Beat Vegas.** Every pick is judged by its price against
the market close, a fixed 10-test ledger at 0.5% each keeps luck from promoting anything, and a
no-edge strategy stops fast. Its limits are sensitivity and bookkeeping, not honesty.

Stated, not decided: **+3% is the smallest edge this design can find in one season.** One week's
evidence is capped by the week-to-week term (variance 0.02² + 0.06²/n): at about 10 picks a week a
+3% week adds about 0.59 to the log-likelihood ratio (promotion at 5.08), a +2% week about 0.26, so
+2% would need about 15-20 weeks. January's "stop" will mean "no edge of 3% or more found".

Verified while briefing (2026-10-09): CFBD files every postseason game as week 1 (2025's "week 1"
runs 2025-08-23 to 2026-01-20), and `scripts/protocol_position.py::completed` keys weeks by
(season, week), so the whole bowl season would enter as one week after the title game; and a week
enters only when every pick in it is graded, so one ungradable pick holds its week as "pending"
for good.

Tate's decisions:
1. **Bookkeeping fixes, each its own PR:** bowl weeks counted separately; a rule so an ungradable
   pick cannot freeze its week; abandoning and restarting a test after a read spends its 0.5%;
   the doc drift (`BETTING_POLICY.md:306-311`, `HEALTH.md` credits, `OPS_ACCOUNTS.md` entries).
2. **Side readings printed beside every Monday read** (the registered statistic is unchanged):
   Hard Rock's offset from the consensus vs the model's own part; the share of readings that
   used a line conversion; the result with BetRivers and Bally Bet counted as one book; the count
   of picks dropped for a 1.5-point move.
3. **The model's training leaks are fixed now and the clocks restarted.** The three arms' clocks
   are **paused before any completed week**: readings count only from the first build on the
   leak-fixed model; picks logged until then are development data.
4. **A restart before any week has been read is free**; a restart after a read spends the slot.
5. **Inputs with no leak-free history** (weather before 2024; same-season roster and talent):
   bring each input with its own options and evidence before deciding.
6. **Register a one-look check of the judge** on 2023-25 first-half data: bet the 48-hour line,
   judge it against the 30-minute close, and see whether the positive-value bets won more.
7. **Start everything now**, in parallel with the review: separate worktrees, separate PRs, each
   with its own approval.

offensive style on decision-time data, 2024-25. R06 was rejected because switching the model to

- **A. History, one pre-registered look, 2024-25 (exploratory: it reports and licenses a paper
  arm, spends no ledger alpha; 2023-25 is spent for derived rules).** Universe: FBS games with a
  real first-half consensus both 48 hours out and at the close, outdoor, with a decision-safe
  forecast 72 hours out (`weather_obs`, available before the bet). Rule: forecast sustained wind at
  or above the frozen threshold. Bet: the under at the 48-hour consensus line. Two readings:
  **line value** against the 30-minute consensus close (is the market slow to price a windy
  forecast?) and **the record against the close**, pushes dropped (does the close still underprice
  it?). Calm games read the same way as a control; a dose-response read of both quantities on
  forecast wind is reported, not a criterion. Full game alongside, record against the close only
  (no 2024-25 full-game early line). Sizing (VERIFIED, counts only, no outcomes read): 186 of the
  2024-25 priced outdoor games had a 72-hour forecast of 10+ mph and both lines; 89 at 12+; 33 at 15+.
- **B. The Salaga look** (approved v1.3): under on observed game weather past the paper's cutoffs,
  2016-2025, one look, in the Holm family of six. Needs `weather_actuals` to reach 2025; run
  37974821053 is fetching (kept running, Tate 2026-10-09).
- **C. A forward paper arm (a ledger slot under p1.1):** at every card build, each outdoor game
  whose newest decision-safe forecast, fetched at the build, passes the threshold → a paper
  first-half under at Hard Rock's main line and price, judged by `ev_cons`. It does not use the
  model, so its clock need not wait for the leak fix. About 6 games a week at 10 mph (more in
  November). Needs forecast capture restarted: `weather_obs` stops at 2026-09-15 (VERIFIED).
- **D. The model meanwhile:** the leaky actual-weather inputs come out of the leak-fixed model
  (the dome flag stays); forecast weather enters the model only through its own gate.

**Tate's calls (2026-10-09): build all four (A, B, C, D); the threshold is a forecast sustained
wind of 10+ mph 72 hours out, frozen for A and C; C runs as two arms, first half and full game
(ledger slots 4 and 5).** Each part is a registry row first, then its own PR.

### The 2026 test design (VERIFIED from `docs/PROTOCOL_2026.md`, `scripts/protocol_sim.py`, `beatvegas/protocol.py`)
- Three of the roadmap's seven arms are registered: H-ARM-1HU, H-ARM-1HO, H-ARM-FGU. "Derivative
  consistency" and "early/stale lines" — the two that would not need the model to out-predict the
  market — have no registry row.
- Each arm: weekly mean `ev_cons`, a sequential test of 0 against +3% expected value, false-pass
  rate 0.5% per arm, power 80%, at least 30 readings over 3 weeks, ends at season end.
- The simulation: a real +3% arm passes 56% of the time at 8 picks a week, 80% at 15; a −5% arm
  stops in about 4 weeks every time. Calendar time limits it more than volume: the week-to-week
  term caps one week's evidence, so a pass needs at least about 5 weeks.
- **Most likely January result: every registered arm stops ("needs more evidence").** A stop
  means "no edge of 3% or more found", not "no edge".
- 54 registry rows to date. Every model, factor, literature and structural test is null; the one
  "positive" (H-FG-U-R) rests on a reading chosen after the numbers. Model error against the
  market: first half 8.34 vs Hard Rock 8.22 (n 213); full game 13.44 vs the close 12.68. Blind
  first-half unders hit 49.5% over 1,902 games; realised minus posted total has a median of 0.000.
- **Seven ways an arm could show a false edge** (topic 3 decides which to fix):
  1. Ranking by gap (Hard Rock line minus model line) selects games where Hard Rock sits above the
     consensus, so an arm can read positive with no model skill. Splitting `ev_cons` into the
     Hard Rock offset and the model's own part would show it.
  2. The half-point table is built from a proxy, and conversions near key numbers feed EV directly.
  3. `far_lines` drops the largest line moves, which is not random.
  4. One ungradable pick silently withholds a whole week (`picks.py:328-332`).
  5. `protocol.weekly` groups by `week` alone; how bowls are keyed is unverified.
  6. "Supersede and restart" is not written as an ending that spends the arm's alpha.
  7. `BETTING_POLICY.md:306-311` says unpriced paper picks take the closing price; the code
     excludes them (`picks.py:354-361`).
  Also: 1HU and FGU share the model's inputs and the week's market move, so two passes would not
  be two independent confirmations. Never shown: that `ev_cons` predicts profit in this market
  (testable on the 2023-25 closes).

### The three videos (transcripts pulled 2026-10-09; LINEMAKER SPORTS)
- "How I Won 86% Of My Sports Betting Picks for 2 Months Straight" (2026-03-20): bet heavy
  favourites, mostly player props; "true probability" is the average of a season hit rate and a
  matchup hit rate; 32-5 in February and 19-3 in March at an average of −366.
- "My Simple 3 Step Betting Strategy That Got Me Rich" (2026-10-07): find the gap between the
  implied and estimated probability, size the risk with fixed units, trust a tracked process.
- "The Only Strategy I'd Use To Get Rich Sports Betting" (2026-09-16): pick bad prices, not
  winners; high probability plus a positive gap; 1-5% units; compound monthly.

All three sell the creator's AI subscription ($49, $99 or $199 a month).

**Checked against the numbers:**
- 51-8 at an average of −366 (break-even 78.5%) has a Wilson 95% interval of 75.5-93.0% and a
  one-sided p of 0.09 against break-even: **not distinguishable from break-even.** The two-month
  window was chosen by the seller; there is no third-party record and no closing-line value.
- "10% ROI a month, like the stock market's 10% a year" mixes return per dollar wagered with
  return on bankroll.
- An averaged hit rate uses the same public numbers the book sets the prop from; a "15% edge" on
  that basis is almost certainly estimation error. At −366, a true 75% instead of 85% is −4.5%.
- A fixed gap is worth less at heavy favourites: +8 points is +16% expected value at 50%, +10.8% at
  74%, +9.4% at 85%.
- Ten times a bankroll in a year at 1% units needs about 21% growth a month: about 210 bets a month
  at +10%, or about 700 at +3%.
- Sound and standard: price over prediction, flat pre-set stakes, never chase, judge the process,
  track everything, check calibration. Never mentioned: closing-line value, line shopping, account
  limits, sample size, the vig on props.

**Against Beat Vegas:** it already does the sound parts, more strictly (line value against the
close, $10 flat with a weekly cap, provenance on every ticket, pre-registered tests, intervals). It
lacks one thing the videos rely on: a calibrated probability per bet. The model returns a line and
a gap in points; `bv_sigma` is one slate-wide number, so the model cannot state its own expected
value or be checked for calibration. College player props, the videos' main market, are excluded
from Hard Rock in Florida by the compact ("not including proposition bets on such collegiate
sport", `docs/LEGAL_MEMO_2026-10.md` §1). Worth a look, not adoption: favourite-longshot bias in
college moneylines (captured since 2026-10-07; literature first).

## Section B3 — grading, leakage and selection bias (VERIFIED by the third sweep, 2026-10-11)

**The two "line value" fields on the site are not what they are described as.**

  late alternate rungs (−140 to −160, 1-1.5 pts lower) can pull the "close" down and flatter an

  moves, in either direction) by construction.
- A −110 default survives in `grading.py:38`, `scripts/grade.py:135,172` (the market ledgers),
